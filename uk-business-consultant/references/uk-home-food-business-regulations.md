# UK Food Business Regulations for Small-Scale/Home-Based Operations

**Research compiled for Rhondda Cynon Taf (South Wales) area**
**Sources:** GOV.UK, Food Standards Agency (FSA), Rhondda Cynon Taf Council, UK legislation
**Date:** July 2026

---

## 1. Cottage Food Laws in the UK — What Can You Sell From Home?

**The UK does NOT have formal "cottage food laws" like the US.** Instead, home-based food businesses are treated the same as any other food business — there is no exemption for small-scale or home producers. You must comply with the same food safety and hygiene regulations regardless of where you operate.

**What you CAN do from home:**
- You CAN run a food business from your home kitchen (it does NOT need to be a separate commercial kitchen)
- You CAN sell baked goods (cakes, bread, pastries), preserves (jams, chutneys), confectionery, cooked meals, and traditional cuisine
- You CAN sell at markets/fairs, online, via social media, or as catering
- The same regulations apply regardless of scale or sales channel

**Key principle:** If you sell, cook, store, handle, prepare, or distribute food regularly to the public (even for free), you are a food business and must register.

## 2. Food Hygiene Certification Requirements

**It is NOT a legal requirement to hold a food hygiene certificate**, but the FSA recommends obtaining one:

> *"It is not compulsory for you to have a food hygiene certificate, but if you are looking to start a food business, we recommend that you pursue a food hygiene qualification to improve your knowledge."* — FSA

**What the law actually requires:**
- You must demonstrate that you have been adequately trained in food hygiene
- Training can be through formal qualifications OR on-the-job training
- You must have a documented Food Safety Management System based on HACCP principles (the FSA's **Safer Food Better Business (SFBB)** pack is the standard tool for small businesses)

**Recommended certification levels:**
- **Level 2 Food Hygiene Certificate** — the standard for anyone handling/preparing food (suitable for home bakers, market stall holders)
- **Level 3 Food Hygiene Certificate** — for supervisors/managers
- **Free online allergy training** is available from the FSA

**Accredited training providers:** The Chartered Institute of Environmental Health (CIEH) and other accredited providers offer these courses (typically £15-30 online).

## 3. Kitchen Registration with Local Council (Rhondda Cynon Taf)

### Food Business Registration (Mandatory)

You **MUST register** as a food business with **Rhondda Cynon Taf County Borough Council** if you sell food to the public — even from home.

**Key facts:**
- Registration is **FREE**
- You must register **at least 28 days before you start trading**
- Registration **cannot be refused**
- You register online via the national portal: **[register.food.gov.uk](https://register.food.gov.uk/new)**
- Register your home address as the business premises

**RCT Council contact for food safety:**
- Search "Rhondda Cynon Taf food business registration" or contact their Environmental Health team
- Phone: General council contact via rctcbc.gov.uk
- The council's Environmental Health department will arrange a food hygiene inspection after registration

### What Happens After Registration

Once registered, local authority officers (Environmental Health Officers / EHO) will visit your home to conduct a **food hygiene inspection**. They will assess:
- Food preparation areas
- Food safety procedures
- Hygiene practices
- Storage and handling
- Waste management

**Inspection rating:** You will receive a **Food Hygiene Rating** (from 0-5 in Wales). This can be displayed and customers may check it online.

### Planning Permission

You may need to check with your **local planning authority** (RCT Council) if:
- You're making major alterations to your home
- You plan to have many customer/delivery visits
- You want to advertise outside your home
- Most home food businesses (baking, preserves) operating without significant changes do NOT need planning permission

### Mortgage/Landlord Permission

You may also need permission from your:
- Mortgage provider or landlord
- Home insurance provider (standard home insurance typically doesn't cover business activities)

## 4. Labelling Requirements for Homemade Food

### Pre-packed Food (sold in packaging before sale)

Labels **MUST** show (minimum x-height 1.2mm font, indelible):

1. **Name of the food** — clear description
2. **List of ingredients** — in descending order of weight, headed "ingredients"
3. **Allergen information** — 14 major allergens must be emphasised (in bold/colour): celery, cereals containing gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, lupin, milk, molluscs, mustard, peanuts, sesame, soybeans, sulphur dioxide/sulphites (>10ppm), tree nuts
4. **Net quantity** — weight/volume (g, kg, ml, L)
5. **Best before or Use by date**
6. **Storage conditions** — e.g., "keep refrigerated" or "store in a cool, dry place"
7. **Name and address of the food business operator** — your name/company and address in the UK
8. **Country of origin or place of provenance** — if not obvious from the name
9. **Any special warnings** — e.g., "may contain nuts"

**All mandatory information must be in the same field of vision** (on pre-packed food).

### Pre-packed for Direct Sale (PPDS) — e.g., cakes wrapped at home

- Must show the **name of the food** and **allergen information** on the packaging
- Full ingredients list recommended but not always required by law for PPDS
- **Allergen information is mandatory** and must be easily accessible

### Loose Foods (sold without packaging — e.g., at a market stall)

- You must provide **allergen information** to customers (verbally or in writing)
- You must display a notice or provide written information about allergens

### Packaging Requirements

- Must use **food-grade packaging** (marked "for food contact" or glass-and-fork symbol)
- Packaging must not transfer contaminants to food
- Written "declaration of compliance" from packaging supplier is required for plastic, ceramic, or cellophane packaging

## 5. Selling at Markets/Fairs vs Online vs Catering

### Markets and Fairs
- You need a **Street Trading Licence** from the local council (RCT) to trade at markets
- You must register as a food business (if not already)
- You need a **Food Hygiene Rating** (your home kitchen inspection covers this)
- Allergen information must be available for loose/pre-packed food
- Some markets may have additional requirements (public liability insurance, specific stall layout)

### Online / Distance Selling (social media, website, etc.)
- Same registration and hygiene requirements apply
- **Allergen information must be provided at TWO stages:**
  1. Before purchase is completed (on website, menu, catalogue)
  2. When food is delivered (allergen stickers, enclosed menu, or verbal)
- Food must be delivered safely — temperature-controlled if needed
- Food-grade packaging suitable for transport
- Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 apply (right to cancel, information requirements)
- Electronic Commerce Regulations 2002 apply

### Catering (preparing food for events/parties)
- Same registration and hygiene requirements
- May need to register each new location or event
- Must have food safety management procedures for off-site preparation
- Temperature control during transport is essential
- Inform your local authority if you significantly change your operating model

## 6. Insurance Requirements

**Insurance is NOT a legal requirement** for food businesses, but it is **strongly recommended**:

### Recommended types of insurance:
1. **Public Liability Insurance** — covers claims from customers who get ill or injured from your food (typically £5-10m cover; most market organisers require this)
2. **Product Liability Insurance** — covers claims related to your specific food products
3. **Business Equipment/Contents Insurance** — covers your business equipment and ingredients (standard home insurance won't cover these)
4. **Home Insurance Amendment** — you must tell your home insurer you're running a business from home; they may amend or increase premiums

**Key point:** Standard home insurance does NOT cover business activities, stock, or customer visits.

**Where to get it:** Association of British Insurers (ABI), British Insurance Brokers' Association (BIBA), or specialist food business insurers (e.g., Shield, FoodInsurance.co.uk, Insure4Food).

## 7. Portuguese/Traditional Cuisine Regulations

There are **NO specific regulations for Portuguese or traditional cuisine** in UK food law. All food businesses must follow the same general food safety regulations regardless of cuisine type. However:

### What applies to Portuguese/Traditional cuisine:
- **Standard HACCP-based food safety management** applies to all food preparation
- **Allergen labelling** is critical — traditional Portuguese ingredients (e.g., cod/bacalhau — fish; pasteis de nata — eggs, milk, gluten; certain sausages/enchidos — may contain allergens) must be labelled
- **Temperature control** applies to any food needing refrigeration (meat, dairy-based dishes)
- **Special rules** apply to:
  - **Meat products** (e.g., presunto, chouriço, alheira) — must follow meat hygiene regulations
  - **Cheese** production (e.g., queijo da serra) — if making cheese from raw milk, there are additional dairy hygiene requirements
  - **Preserves and canned goods** — specific rules apply if canning/preserving for long-term storage
- **Importing ingredients** — if importing Portuguese ingredients (e.g., specific cheeses, meats), follow import/export requirements

### Language on Labels
- Labels can be in English only for sale in Wales/UK (Welsh language is an option but not mandatory for domestic sales)
- Traditional names of products can be kept (e.g., "Pastéis de Nata", "Bacalhau à Brás")

## 8. Market Stall Licensing in South Wales

### Street Trading Licence

In **Rhondda Cynon Taf**, to trade at markets or on the street, you typically need a **Street Trading Licence** from the council:

- Apply via **RCT Council's Licensing department**
- Licence types: may distinguish between market traders and street traders
- Fees vary by council — contact RCT directly for current fees
- Food businesses may also need a **Food Business Registration** (separate from the trading licence)

### Market-Specific Requirements

Many markets in South Wales (e.g., Pontypridd Market, farmers' markets, craft fairs) have their own rules:
- Some markets require you to have **Public Liability Insurance** (£5m minimum common)
- Stall hygiene requirements (hand washing facilities, surface cleaning)
- Food temperature control equipment (chilled display if selling perishables)
- May require a **Food Hygiene Rating** certificate visible to customers
- Bookings and fees set by individual market organisers or the council

### Trading Standards

South Wales market traders must comply with:
- Weights and Measures regulations (accurate scales)
- Trading Standards (product descriptions, pricing, age-restricted sales)
- Waste disposal requirements

## 9. What You Can Legally Do Without a Commercial Kitchen

**You do NOT need a separate commercial kitchen** to run a home food business in the UK. Your domestic kitchen can be used, provided:

### Requirements for Home Kitchen:

1. **Clean and maintained in good repair** — surfaces, walls, floors in good condition
2. **Separate workspace** — your food preparation area should be segregated from general household activities where possible
3. **Proper hand washing** — dedicated hand wash basin with hot/cold running water, soap, and hand-drying facilities
4. **Temperature-controlled storage** — adequate fridge/freezer capacity, thermometers
5. **Proper ventilation** — extraction to handle cooking odours and steam
6. **Adequate lighting** — for safe food preparation
7. **Suitable work surfaces** — easy-to-clean, non-porous surfaces (stainless steel or equivalent)
8. **Pest control** — proofed against pests, no signs of infestation
9. **Waste management** — proper food waste disposal, not down the sink

### What the EHO Will Check:
- Separation of raw and ready-to-eat food
- Cleanliness of the kitchen overall
- Personal hygiene practices
- Food storage (labelled, dated, covered)
- Cross-contamination prevention
- Cleaning schedule
- Food safety management system (SFBB pack)
- Waste disposal (fat, oil, grease management — NOT down the sink)

### What You CANNOT Do Without:
- You CAN use your home kitchen legally — but it **must be registered**
- You CANNOT avoid inspection — the EHO will visit once you register
- You CANNOT avoid having a documented food safety management system

### Practical Considerations:
- Pets should be kept out of the kitchen during food preparation
- Consider separate fridge/freezer space for business ingredients
- Avoid cross-contamination with household food
- You can claim a proportion of household bills (heating, lighting, water) as business expenses for Self Assessment

## Key Takeaways

1. **There are no cottage food exemptions** — all home food businesses in the UK must register and be inspected
2. **Registration is free** — register with RCT Council via register.food.gov.uk at least 28 days before trading
3. **You CAN use your home kitchen** — a commercial kitchen is not required, but it must be clean, well-maintained, and pass EHO inspection
4. **Food hygiene certificate is recommended but not mandatory** — though most market organisers and councils expect at least Level 2
5. **Insurance is not legally required but strongly recommended** — most market organisers require public liability insurance
6. **Allergen labelling is mandatory** — for all forms of sale (pre-packed, PPDS, loose)
7. **No special regulations for Portuguese cuisine** — standard food safety rules apply to all cuisines
8. **Separate Street Trading Licence may be needed** for market/fair trading from RCT Council
9. **Online selling requires allergen info at two stages** (before purchase and on delivery)
10. **Plan for the EHO visit** — get a Safer Food Better Business pack and set up proper procedures before registering
