# UK Micro-Startup Guide: Food Business on £500–£1,000

Research compiled from official GOV.UK sources (July 2026).  
Covers: sole trader vs limited company, HMRC registration, tax, NI, VAT, expenses, record-keeping, business banking, and side-hustle/gift economy rules for very small food operations.

---

## 1. Sole Trader vs Limited Company — Which at This Scale?

### Sole Trader (Recommended for £500–£1k micro-startup)

| Factor | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Setup cost** | £0 — free to register for Self Assessment |
| **Complexity** | Simplest structure. Minimal paperwork. |
| **Liability** | Unlimited — you're personally responsible for all debts |
| **Tax** | Pay Income Tax + NI on profits through Self Assessment |
| **Registration** | Register for Self Assessment if earning > £1,000/yr. Can start trading immediately without registering. |
| **Accounts** | Simple records of income & expenses. No need to file with Companies House. |

**Source:** [gov.uk/become-sole-trader](https://www.gov.uk/become-sole-trader)

### Limited Company

| Factor | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Setup cost** | £12–£50+ to register with Companies House (online £12). Ongoing costs for filing. |
| **Complexity** | Must file annual accounts, confirmation statements, Corporation Tax returns. |
| **Liability** | Limited — personal assets are protected; you're only liable up to your investment |
| **Tax** | Company pays 19% Corporation Tax on profits. You then pay Income Tax + NI on salary/dividends taken out. |
| **Registration** | Must register BEFORE trading. Can't start without being incorporated. |
| **Admin burden** | Higher — Companies House filings, director responsibilities, corporation tax. |

**Source:** [gov.uk/limited-company-formation](https://www.gov.uk/limited-company-formation)

### Verdict

**At £500–£1,000 startup budget, sole trader is the clear choice.** Reasons:

- **No setup cost** — £0 to start, compared to £12+ for a limited company
- **Zero admin overhead** — no Companies House filings, no Corporation Tax returns
- **You won't hit the point where limited company tax efficiency matters** (that's typically above ~£30k–£40k profit where salary/dividend splitting saves meaningful tax)
- **If your food business is very small (side-hustle level), sole trader is the default** most people use
- **Unlimited liability risk** is real but manageable with proper insurance (public liability insurance ~£50–£100/yr)
- A limited company's £12 registration + potential accountant fees would eat 2–10% of your entire startup capital

**Switch to limited company later** when profits consistently exceed ~£20k–£30k and you want liability protection or tax efficiency on retained profits.

## 2. Registering with HMRC

### The £1,000 Trading Allowance (The Most Important Rule for Micro-Businesses)

- You have a **£1,000 tax-free trading allowance** per tax year (6 April – 5 April)
- If your total income from self-employment is **£1,000 or less**, you don't need to register or tell HMRC
- If it's **over £1,000**, you must register for Self Assessment and file a tax return
- **You can't use the trading allowance AND claim expenses** — you use one or the other (whichever gives the best result)
- For a micro food startup: if your total revenue is under £1k, you likely owe £0 tax and don't need to register

**Example:** If you earn £800 total from selling baked goods in a year: no need to register, no tax due.

### When You Must Register

- If your gross income **exceeds £1,000** in a tax year, you must:
  1. **Register for Self Assessment** by 5 October after the tax year ends
  2. **File a Self Assessment tax return** by 31 January
  3. **Pay any tax due** by 31 January

**Source:** [gov.uk/become-sole-trader](https://www.gov.uk/become-sole-trader) — "You must register for Self Assessment as a sole trader if you earn more than £1,000 in a tax year"

### How to Register

1. Go to [gov.uk/register-for-self-assessment](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-self-assessment)
2. Click "Start now" — you'll get a **Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR)** within 10 working days
3. You can start trading immediately. Registration can be done after you start, before 5 October following the end of the tax year.

### Key Deadlines

| Event | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Register for Self Assessment | 5 October after tax year ends |
| File online tax return | 31 January |
| Pay tax owed | 31 January |
| Tax year runs | 6 April – 5 April |

## 3. Self Assessment Tax Returns

- You file one annual return covering your self-employment income
- The system uses **cash basis** by default from 2024/25 — you record income when you RECEIVE it and expenses when you PAY them (ideal for micro businesses)
- HMRC calculates what you owe based on your return
- You can file online via [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/log-in-file-self-assessment-tax-return)
- **Penalties** apply for late filing or late payment

### Income Tax Rates (2026/27)

| Band | Taxable Profit | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| Personal Allowance | £0 – £12,570 | 0% |
| Basic Rate | £12,571 – £50,270 | 20% |
| Higher Rate | £50,271 – £125,140 | 40% |
| Additional Rate | Over £125,140 | 45% |

**Source:** [gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns](https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns)

## 4. National Insurance Contributions (2026/27)

### Class 2 NI

| Profits | What You Pay |
|---|---|
| **Less than £7,105/yr** | £0 (but can pay voluntary contributions at £3.65/week to protect NI record) |
| **£7,105 or more** | Treated as paid — you don't actually pay but contributions are credited |

### Class 4 NI

| Profits | Rate |
|---|---|
| **Less than £12,570/yr** | £0 |
| **£12,570 – £50,270** | 6% on profits above £12,570 |
| **Over £50,270** | 2% on profits above £50,270 |

**Source:** [gov.uk/self-employed-national-insurance-rates](https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-national-insurance-rates)

### Practical impact for a micro-food startup

- If your **profits are under £7,105**: you pay £0 NI
- If profits are under £12,570: you pay £0 NI (only Class 2 treated as paid)
- For a £500–£1k startup you're very likely well under all NI thresholds in the first year or two

## 5. VAT Threshold & When It Matters

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Current threshold** | £90,000 taxable turnover |
| **Does it apply to you?** | Almost certainly not — a £500–£1k startup is nowhere near this |
| **Voluntary registration?** | Possible but pointless at this scale — adds admin burden with no benefit |
| **Food-specific note** | Most food is **zero-rated for VAT** (basic groceries, cakes, biscuits, etc.), some is **standard-rated** (confectionery, ice cream, hot takeaways). If you're selling zero-rated food, VAT registration has minimal benefit since you can't charge VAT to customers but can reclaim input VAT. |

**Source:** [gov.uk/how-vat-works](https://www.gov.uk/how-vat-works) — thresholds from this page.

## 6. Allowable Expenses (Food Business Specific)

### You CAN Claim

| Expense Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Ingredients & stock** | All raw materials, food ingredients, packaging supplies |
| **Equipment** | Ovens, mixers, scales, baking trays, thermometers, knives (capital allowances on items over £200 or claim as expense if using cash basis) |
| **Home office** | Proportion of heating, electricity, council tax, rent/mortgage interest, broadband, phone |
| **Kitchen costs (home-run)** | Proportion of gas/electric used for cooking; cleaning products; kitchen equipment depreciation |
| **Business insurance** | Public liability, product liability, equipment insurance |
| **Marketing** | Website hosting, domain, social media ads, printed menus/flyers |
| **Transport** | Fuel for deliveries, bus/train fares for market trips |
| **Packaging** | Boxes, bags, labels, stickers, jars |
| **Training** | Food hygiene certificates (Level 2 Food Safety), allergen training |
| **Licensing costs** | Food business registration fee, market stall fees |
| **Professional fees** | Accountant fees, bookkeeping software costs |
| **Clothing** | Uniforms, aprons, hairnets, gloves — protective clothing for food prep |
| **Bank charges** | Business account fees |

### Home Office Simplified Expenses

Rather than working out exact proportions, you can claim **simplified flat rates**:

| Hours worked from home per month | Flat rate per month |
|---|---|
| 25 – 50 | £10 |
| 51 – 100 | £18 |
| 101+ | £26 |

Or do a **room/proportion calculation**:
- Count rooms in your home
- Work out percentage used for business
- Apply to bills (heating, electricity, etc.)
- Then pro-rate for time spent (e.g., 5 days/week = 5/7 of that proportion)

**Key note:** "You cannot claim expenses if you use your £1,000 tax-free trading allowance." — GOV.UK. If you claim expenses, you forgo the trading allowance. If you claim the trading allowance, you forgo expenses. Choose whichever gives the lower taxable profit.

**Source:** [gov.uk/expenses-if-youre-self-employed](https://www.gov.uk/expenses-if-youre-self-employed)

## 7. Record-Keeping Requirements

### What You Must Keep

- **Income records**: invoices issued, till receipts, bank deposit records, PayPal/Stripe records
- **Expense records**: receipts for every purchase (ingredients, equipment, packaging, etc.)
- **Bank statements** from your business account
- **Personal income records** if you have other income too

### How Long to Keep Records

- **5 years** from the 31 January submission deadline of the relevant tax year

### Accounting Method

- **Cash basis is default** from 2024/25 tax year — record income when money hits your account, record expenses when you pay them. This is ideal for micro businesses.

### Recommended Approach

| Method | Tool | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Simple spreadsheet | Google Sheets / Excel | Free |
| Free accounting | Wave / GNUCash | Free |
| Included with bank | FreeAgent (with Mettle) | Free with Mettle account |
| Basic bookkeeping | Starling "Spaces" | Free |

**Source:** [gov.uk/self-employed-records](https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-records)

## 8. Business Bank Accounts

### Do You NEED One?

- **Legally no** — sole traders can use a personal account
- **Practically yes** — HMRC expects clear separation. Mixing business and personal makes record-keeping a nightmare

### Comparison: Best for Micro Businesses

| Bank | Monthly Fee | Key Features | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starling** | **£0** | No monthly fees, free UK payments, instant notifications, tax "Spaces" pots, digital receipts, integrates with accounting tools | Best all-round free option |
| **Mettle (NatWest)** | **£0** | FreeAgent accounting software included (normally ~£150/yr), invoicing, tax calculation, savings pots | Best value if you want free accounting software built in |
| **Monzo Business** | £0 (free tier) or £5/mo (paid tier) | Free tier available, paid tier adds invoicing, accounting integrations | Good if you already use Monzo personally |

**Starling details:** [starlingbank.com/business-account](https://www.starlingbank.com/business-account/) — 500,000+ business accounts, FSCS protected, ATM withdrawals free, deposit cash at Post Office (0.7% fee).

**Mettle details:** [mettle.co.uk](https://www.mettle.co.uk/) — FreeAgent included, sole traders and limited companies with up to 2 owners, balance limit up to £1m.

**Note:** All three accounts are free to open. With a £500–£1000 budget, a free business account is the only sensible choice.

## 9. Gift Economy & Side-Hustle Rules for Very Small Food Ops

### The Trading Allowance (£1,000)

This is the most important rule for "gift economy" or very small operations:

- If your total income from the food business is **under £1,000** in a tax year: **£0 tax, no registration needed**
- This covers scenarios like: friends paying for baked goods, "pay what you feel" sales at community events, small market stalls
- HMRC uses the term "trading" — if you sell regularly to make a profit, you're trading. If it's truly casual (occasional bake sale for charity, giving food to friends who happen to contribute), you may not be trading at all

### When HMRC Considers You "Trading"

You're likely trading if you:
- Sell regularly to make a profit
- Make items specifically to sell for profit
- Sell on a regular basis (online, at markets, through classified ads)
- Earn commission from selling goods for others
- Are paid for a service you provide

You're likely **NOT** trading if:
- You only occasionally sell items
- It's a genuine hobby with no profit motive
- You give food away and people happen to donate
- It's a one-off charity event

### Gift Economy Specifics

- If you operate on a "gift" or "pay what you feel" basis, HMRC looks at **actual economic reality**, not the label. If money changes hands regularly in exchange for food, that's income.
- **Donations vs sales**: If you clearly label it as donations for a charity or community project with no expectation of food in return, different rules apply. But if someone pays and gets a specific food item, that's a sale.
- The **£1,000 trading allowance** effectively makes the first £1,000 of gift-economy income tax-free without even needing to register.

### Food Business Registration — Mandatory Regardless

Even if you're under the £1k trading threshold, if you're selling/cooking/preparing food for others, you MUST:

1. **Register as a food business** with your local authority at least 28 days before trading
2. Follow food safety and hygiene regulations
3. Complete a Level 2 Food Hygiene certificate (recommended, often required by local authority)
4. Follow allergen labelling rules

This is separate from HMRC/tax rules. Food safety law applies regardless of income level.

**Source:** [gov.uk/guidance/food-business-registration](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/food-business-registration) — "You must register with your local authority to run a food business."

## 10. Step-by-Step Action Plan (£500–£1k Food Startup)

### Phase 1: Setup (£0 cost)

1. ✅ Complete a **Level 2 Food Safety and Hygiene** certificate (~£12–20 online) — mandatory knowledge
2. ✅ **Register as a food business** with your local council (free) — do this 28 days before you start
3. ✅ **Open a business bank account** (Starling or Mettle — both free)
4. ✅ Get **public liability insurance** (~£50–100/yr for micro food businesses)

### Phase 2: Trading Below £1,000

5. ✅ Start trading. You can do this immediately without registering with HMRC as long as income stays under £1k
6. ✅ Keep simple records of all income and expenses anyway (good habit)
7. ✅ Don't use the £1k trading allowance if you have significant expenses — instead claim expenses if they exceed £1k

### Phase 3: Trading Above £1,000

8. **Register for Self Assessment** on gov.uk by 5 October after the tax year
9. **File Self Assessment** online by 31 January
10. Pay any Income Tax and NI due by 31 January

### Phase 4: Growing Beyond Side-Hustle

11. Consider switching to limited company when profits exceed £20k–£30k
12. Consider VAT registration when turnover approaches £90k
13. Invest in proper accounting software
